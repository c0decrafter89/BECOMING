# BECOMING
## The Ghost-Jurisdiction of a Life
### An autobiography, 1989–2026, read through *The Ghost-Jurisdiction: Language, Relation, and the Recovery of Being*

> *A person is not made real by a document, a category, a creed, a border, or an authorized vocabulary. Personhood precedes its administrative description.*
> — *The Ghost-Jurisdiction*

> *I exist. I continue. I'm here now.*

---

## How to Read This

There's an earlier version of this story in `drafts/autobiography-draft-1.md`. It tells what happened, year by year, with a timeline, a work history, and a list of every place I've slept. This version tells the same life again, underpinned by a framework I keep in `frameworks/the-ghost-jurisdiction.md`.

That framework asks one question I've been circling for thirty-seven years without the words for it: **what happens to a person in the gap between who they are and how they've been described?** I've been described a lot: as a good Mormon boy, a missionary, a husband, a pothead, a patient, a diagnosis, a defendant, a transient, a lot guy, a gay man (for about a week). Some of those descriptions were true for a while. None of them was the whole of me. The framework calls the space where the rest of a person goes on living the **Ghost-Jurisdiction**: *the domain of presence that persists when formal recognition fails.*

I want to use the framework honestly, the way it asks to be used. Its terms are lenses, not proofs. *Spell*, *paper-genocide*, *heart-off statehood*, *truepulse*: these name patterns I've lived inside. They don't prove that any church, hospital, or court was conspiring against me. The framework is careful about that, and I've learned I need to be careful too, because I've spent a lot of my life turning metaphors into enemies. It also warns that **critique must not reproduce the collective blame it opposes**. So this is not a story about who did it to me. It's a story about how a person gets lost inside descriptions, and how he begins, slowly, to speak for himself without making his own words into a new cage.

## Threshold: Before the File
*1989*

The first paper I ever had was a birth certificate: **September 2, 1989, Portland, Oregon.** It records a name, a date, two parents. It doesn't record that my parents met in Provo, my father a BYU student who never finished, my mother a Chilean immigrant who'd come down from Rexburg. It doesn't record that her father brought the family into the Mormon church in the 1960s, part faith and part ladder for a family making a life in a new country. It doesn't record that I was conceived in Utah and born in Oregon because my mother's sister had already moved here. It doesn't record my brother Quinton waiting at home, or the Indian doctor whose hands delivered me.

*Every being enters relation before it enters a registry.* I was somebody's son, somebody's brother, somebody's nephew, before I was a number.

My earliest memory is a ghost in the plainest sense the framework gives: *memory beyond the record.* I remember my parents fighting and my mother jumping from a ledge and breaking her leg. Both of them have told me I wasn't there. The family record says I wasn't present. My body says I was. I've stopped needing to settle it. What I'm sure of is what lived under it: a small child who didn't feel safe and whose body learned to stay braced. I would carry that stored-up adrenaline into every chapter that follows.

## I. Ground and Enclosure
*1989–2001 · Ages 0–12*

> *The search for ground is among the deepest movements of human thought… The trap begins when a single authority claims the exclusive right to define ground.*

### The search for ground

When I was three or four, my mother met Rich, a former hockey goalie who missed the 1980 Olympics through injury and went on to build homes around Portland. He became my stepfather and gave me a lot of my ground: soccer from five to thirteen (the Fire Ants, the Purple People Leaders, Team Ice), the discipline of *run a lap when the coach says run a lap*, and, at his drafting table on Browns Drive, the patient lesson of how to tape a page down flat, one corner at a time, smoothing it with your hand. Every Mother's Day he took us all to brunch. I couldn't feel it then. I can now.

Ground was also the houses: First Place in Hillsboro, where I went from sharing a room with Quinton to having my own, and then, around 1998, the house on Browns Drive off Laurel and Bald Peak, with its creaking lever-operated elevator that felt to me like the elevator in *Charlie and the Great Glass Elevator*, going down to go up. Ground was my cousin Matt. Ground was my brother Nick, born when I was six. Ground was the video store, where my family practiced its truest liturgy. Movies were close to sacred in our house, and I could stand at our VHS cabinet for ten minutes just reading the spines and dreaming.

### The first enclosure

When I was five, at a neighbor's house, an older child sexually abused me. I buried it so deep that for years I told a different story about my "first time," even to myself.

The framework says an enclosure need not have walls. It can be built from what follows a person wherever they go. Shame was the first wall I carried. I had no words for what happened, so I had no way to put it down. It sped me past innocence before I had language, and it taught me the most important lesson of my childhood: **hide**. The boy had Legos. Legos can still set me off.

### The panopticon carried inside

> *Under that possibility, people begin to monitor themselves… The observer need not be present in every room. The rules have been carried inside.*

By fourth grade, in Mr. Merrill's class at Farmington View, I had built what I'd later call a **social automaton**. *Blink when they say blink. Jump when they say jump. Copy and please people, and nothing bad can happen.* I copied Mr. Merrill's tic and hoped no one noticed. I had no adult I trusted, never opened up, never asked questions, and had already made a lot of conclusions. I was a Jekyll-and-Hyde kid.

That's the framework's panopticon exactly: not a guard tower, but *the felt possibility of being interpreted at every moment by a gaze whose location is uncertain.* No one was watching me as closely as I watched myself. I performed the identity most likely to pass through the gate.

Even then I sometimes saw the stitching. *Things seem a lot. Seam a lot.* The world looked like a fabric, and I could see where it was sewn. At the indoor soccer arena I'd walk in loops past the arcade games, hoping that if I circled long enough I'd wake up somewhere else. At the Belts' house, while the grown-ups talked about analog versus digital phones, I drifted to the computer room. I don't know where I went. I went.

### My father's spell

On weekends I went to my father's. He insisted on Christmas Eve at his apartment. He told us he loved us, but around him I felt *icky and unsafe*. Almost every weekend he taught me the same sentence: his life had gone wrong because of a **chemical imbalance**, his ADHD, and so it wasn't his fault.

The framework would call that a spell: *a patterned utterance that makes an outcome imaginable, repeatable, and socially operative.* Not magic, just repetition backed by a father's authority. It taught me that if something is broken inside you, nothing is yours to answer for. Years later, when a hospital wrote a word into my file, I already knew how to live inside it.

On September 11, 2001, I was twelve, in Ms. Hoffmeister's sixth grade. I remember the coverage in my parents' room: *loud in a silent, supernatural way.*

## II. The Spell
*2002–2008 · Ages 13–19*

> *A promise binds; a verdict changes a person's standing; a label directs attention; a command organizes bodies. Words are not only mirrors.*

### Two tongues

At J.B. Thomas Middle School I started living in two vocabularies. At church I was one person; at home and school, another. I formed a band with Wil Koehnke, a friend I'd met at Farmington View, and our friends Nick and Rob, and later Wil's brother Kevin on drums. We played from seventh grade until my mission: a barn show where people said we sounded like the Doors, a battle of the bands we won and then lost the next year to a band called Couch. I quit soccer at thirteen, because soccer asked me to be in my body, and being in my body meant getting close to what happened when I was five.

Eighth grade was punk: long hair, Sid Vicious, Listerine before school until I threw up in class. I pushed away a girl who liked me, Karina Hoffman. I think I already hated myself. Then, around fourteen and three-quarters, I switched tongues entirely and became a **youthful Mormon zealot**: early-morning seminary, memorized scripture, the A student, the perfect citizen.

### The engineering of the sayable

The framework says a grammar *makes some relations easy to express and others awkward or unthinkable.* Mormonism gave me a complete grammar. It told me what to say and what I didn't have to say. Above all, it meant I never had to choose. Someone else was driving. Leaders praised me, and the praise kept the battery charged. I called the place I lived **sky city**: an untouchable bubble with no bullies and no friction. It felt like freedom. I've come to believe it kept me from growing. With nothing to push against, I didn't go through the turmoil that builds a person.

Inside the spell, everything worked. I busked Dylan and Johnny Cash at the Saturday Market in a brown felt hat with my father's old Silvertone guitar and sometimes made over a hundred dollars. I swapped the transmission in a 1988 Honda Civic at sixteen and got a job at Taco Time, and Wil followed me there. I put on a gray beard and did a Moses doomsday monologue over the lunchroom intercom and got applause. I learned the most dangerous lesson of the storm years: *you can fool around through life and it will all work out.* I believed that until 2020.

Underneath, the costs piled up. My grandmother came to live with us, on oxygen, missing her husband, and I gave her my room and left her alone while I played guitar behind a closed door. I mostly didn't eat lunch. I provoked Quinton until he threw me down and broke my collarbone, and then I held it against him. That was a trap I set my whole life: needle someone until they get physical, then turn the other cheek. I heated metal rods to burn a wart off my hand, thinking *kill the alien*. My body didn't respond the first time a girl, Sadie Lopez, wanted more from me, and I walked away instead of wondering why. I dreamed I fell into a pit with a rope leading out, forgot the rope, and watched it disappear. My grades slid from straight A's to coasting.

### Living-ghost language I didn't hear

> *Living-ghost language names speech that remembers what official categories leave out… a song, a joke, a place-name, a gesture.*

Wil saw through me. In seventh grade we'd paged through his mother's book of sex illustrations and laughed at a drawing of a man masturbating. His laugh was real. Mine was hiding something. He never forgot it. He wrote a song with the line ***"You may be a robot, but I'm not a toy,"*** and another called **"The Storm"**: *there's a storm in the distance, where you gonna go?*

That's what the framework calls *high-frequency, low-entropy transmission*: a small sign carrying a large understanding, between people with a shared history. Wil was telling me what he saw in the only language our friendship allowed. I played those songs dozens of times and never once asked him what he meant. The message was sent. I wasn't there to receive it.

### Paper that said more than I'd done

I made Eagle Scout at eighteen by clearing weeds at Rood Bridge Park with volunteers recruited through a church notice. It was so easy it felt like a lie, and at the banquet I'd photoshopped my face onto a presidential portrait. I graduated in 2008, recorded an album of devotional songs ("This Is His Story," "Crystal Mountain View," "The Storm Out There"), and imagined selling it at the BYU bookstore. Listening back, the lyrics confess what I couldn't: the faith was, for me, *a pretty painting on the wall.*

I applied to BYU and for a mission because those were the sentences already written for me. Nobody stopped to ask whether I was actually there.

## III. The Beguiler Turned Inside Out
*2008–2015 · Ages 19–25*

> *Who receives the power to define deception? Who is presumed honest before speaking, and who must prove that their difference is harmless?*

### Carrying the authorized tongue

I went to Sonora, Mexico, as a missionary, so fluent in Spanish that I only needed three weeks at the training center, and proud of it. For about two years, in Hermosillo, Sonoita, Obregón, and Guaymas, I was the one carrying the authorized vocabulary into other people's homes. I taught that they needed fixing and that I, a priesthood holder, knew how.

The framework turns the figure of the beguiler inside out, and I have to turn it on myself first. I was presumed honest before I spoke. I was the light on the hill. And I was masking poverty anxiety and hatred I didn't know I had, overriding my body the way we were trained to, for the sake of the mission. I screamed in English at a companion, Elder Niquet, when he sat on my bed and wouldn't move. He had found the edge of my fairy-tale character, and I ignored what that told me.

I baptized people: Jesús Tenorio, a trucker who hauled dynamite, and his family; Patricia and her daughters; Chano in Obregón; Felipe Bello, a stonemason, in a leaking font where we baptized his family sitting down; a family who'd fled Juárez; a boy named Luis; two brothers, Oswaldo and Enrique, whose mother rejoiced for them. Later I'd feel I'd sold them a product I didn't believe. The framework adds a correction I need: *a community's theology may be rich, internally diverse, and sincerely oriented toward care.* Those families' faith belongs to them. It isn't mine to rename.

### Rootflesh

> *Rootflesh joins embodiment to place without claiming that a person's identity can be reduced to ancestry, blood, or territory.*

Back in Provo from 2011, I taught Spanish to new missionaries and sleepwalked through classes. In **winter 2012** I went to **Bolivia** with my friend Matt Moen to teach employment workshops, and something in me touched ground I hadn't known I had. I'm Andean on my mother's side. In La Paz our neighbors, Lawrence and Heidi Chávez, made me feel I belonged, and Heidi reminded me that my mother was from Chile. I saw Potosí, Oruro, Tarija, and Sucre. I danced until dawn in Oruro. During Carnival, my mother's sister took us from Cobija into Brazil, and I met my cousins Luna, Simón, and Valentina for the first time. *Rootflesh.* Not an identity I could claim by blood, but a relation I could feel in my body.

Even there, I was teaching others to recite *power statements* about themselves, and I had a dermatologist laser the redness from my face. I was always flushed. I think now it was shame.

That summer I ran the Deseret News Marathon in about three and a half hours, and I met Nictehai Martínez, who made me eggs and kissed me on her doorstep.

### A formula that bound

> *A religious or civic formula may bind participants to a shared promise.*

I met **Olivia** in my ward. She called me "bud" the first time I knocked on her door. We married in the temple in **June 2013**. The ceremony's language was so powerful it brought me to tears, while my mother and Rich, who'd raised me, waited outside because they weren't allowed in. We honeymooned at Disneyland and posed in front of a *happily ever after* mural.

The formula bound us, but it couldn't make me ready. I couldn't hold a job: a lab position where I built nothing for months, a freelance site I never worked on. I started smoking pot and mistook dissociation for wisdom. I lied to Olivia about it, enjoyed getting away with it, and brought her down into it with me. The one time I clearly remember us making love, after getting high in a hotel in eastern Oregon, I said cruel things in the middle of it, and she laughed as though it were a joke. It's still a sad core memory.

At a church meeting a man said *stay the course; don't deviate*. I heard it as a prophecy in reverse.

### Naming myself the beguiler

> *When the unfamiliar is repeatedly described as a threat in disguise, the description may reveal as much about the boundaries of the speaker's order as about the people being named.*

In **October 2014**, at Disneyland again, dressed as Thor and out of my mind, I told Olivia I wanted a divorce. I told her it was me, that she'd always be my angel. Months later she asked if she still was, and I told her I'd made that up. We divorced in **November 2014**. I wrote to her once explicitly and once anonymously afterward. She asked me never to contact her again. She's remarried now, to a man with a daughter.

The religion I'd been raised in has a phrase about *angels of darkness appearing as light.* After I left the marriage, I started applying it to myself. I became, in my own mind, a **Satanist**, a closet beguiler, an evil magician who let people reap what they sowed. The framework describes how *suspicion becomes a social technology: the person is judged not by what they have done, but by what their category is said to imply.* I did that to myself. I took the darkest category my tradition offered and moved into it.

The framework also asks me to keep a distinction I've often dropped: a tradition's many people are not the same as its institutions' practices. Olivia was not the institution. My parents, who sat outside the temple doors and later greeted her kindly when they ran into her at Nordstrom, were not the institution. Kevin Fudge, my old roommate, an ROTC leader with real ground under him, was not the institution. When I turned my anger into blame for "the Mormons" as a whole, I made the same move I was angry about.

That last winter at BYU I checked out stacks of books I never read, looking for the exit. Two of them gave me something. A Native American prayer book said, in essence: ***in your greatest fear, look to one star.*** A pamphlet, *The Truth of the Rastafarians*, told me I didn't have to throw the baby out with the bathwater. I took some acid by the Provo River. I never finished my history capstone. In **summer 2015** my parents drove out to bring me home.

## IV. Paper
*2015–2019 · Ages 26–29*

> *A paper can protect… The same medium can exclude when a person cannot obtain the document, when the document is treated as more real than the person, or when a category forces a false choice.*

### A word in a file

In **October 2015** I flew one-way to an entrepreneurship conference in New York, telling myself I'd hitchhike home. I was stranded at JFK for three or four days. Nobody answered my GoFundMe, because I acted like everything was fine. I walked onto the highway on the wrong side. Paramedics asked if I wanted to see someone about my mental health, and I thought they meant a counselor. I ended up on the youth ward at **Long Island Jewish**. There I told staff about what happened when I was five, and they listened. I treated it like another trip. I left with a psychiatric diagnosis in my records that no one ever sat down and explained to me.

The framework names something here: *a living person… made difficult to recognize because the system's documents do not have a place for them, or recognize them only in forms that deny their own account of themselves.* It calls this, metaphorically, **paper-genocide**: administrative disappearance. I want to be precise, the way the framework is. I wasn't erased. I was *written down*, and from then on the writing traveled ahead of me. It shaped how each new clinic, shelter, and jail would read me. That began what I think of as ten years of psychiatric oversight, from 2015 to 2025.

I also have to say what the framework would want said: I helped write that file. I learned that certain words got me shelter, attention, a bed, a way out of a jail cell. I've called much of my involvement with mental health *malingering*, and I believe some of it was. I also believe I was genuinely lost. Both can be true. The file was incomplete. That doesn't make it entirely false.

### The lost years

Back home on Tumblestone Drive, I took more than I gave: smoking in my room, stealing from my brother, substitute teaching for nothing, a shoving match with Rich, who was trying to be patient with a son who seemed too far gone. In **2016**, wanting to be seen more than wanting to die, I hung myself from a thin rope, knowing Rich would come. He pulled me down. That year I slept twice with a pregnant woman I'd met in the psych ward. It wasn't love. I was looking for a trip. That year I first heard of Falun Dafa. **2017** I don't remember. I've stopped pretending I do.

### Papers that un-name

In **2018**, back in Provo, I worked at McDonald's and Cougar Pizza and was reported to my sober-living house. I spent a summer with an aunt in Nebraska. Then I helped my father pack up his hoarded life to move home to his parents. In **Hood River** I got out of his car, slammed the door, and flipped him off as he drove away.

I stayed in Hood River, homeless, sleeping on a blanket in a gravel pit among other people living outside: Randall, Chase, Jay, an old man who just wanted enough to drink to be fine for the day, and two skater kids, Elias and Riker, who checked on me. I dumped out bottled water from in front of Safeway to return for joint money. I tried to steal eight dollars of things from Walmart, including a Halloween mask, and spent about ten days in the **Wasco County jail** around Halloween. Once the diagnosis caught up with me there, I asked to move to the psych unit because it was less frightening. Paper, again, doing two things at once: labeling me, and sheltering me.

Back in Hillsboro I moved in with a man called Douglas, who turned out to be living under a borrowed name. His real name was Will Mullins. Paper and person, pulled apart. And late that year I used a website to **remove my own name from the records of the Mormon church.** The temple had taught me that breaking my covenants would put me under Satan's power, and I believed it. In Douglas's mirror I saw my own face, cross-eyed and unfamiliar, and was terrified. I'd un-named myself on paper, but I hadn't yet found the person underneath.

### The paper that protects

There's a harder page here. Sometime after Hood River, my father was convicted of sexual contact with his stepdaughter, a minor. He's incarcerated, likely for the rest of his life.

For years I talked about the law as a *legal hallucination*, a fiction that circumscribes bodies. The framework won't let me use it that way here, and I'm grateful. It says plainly that a paper can *provide evidence against an abusive authority.* A conviction record is paper. So is the protection it gives a child. I know what it is to be a child no record protected. I can hold my own complicated grief for my father and still say: that paper exists for her, and it should.

### 2019: the lot

In 2019 I worked as a lot attendant at **Royal Moore**, a certified pre-owned dealership, filled a storage unit with Goodwill purchases I called treasure, and found the teaching of **Mooji**: *there is nothing to fix, nothing wrong, only the vast presence of here and now.* I lived with Douglas, then with Yarrow in Aloha, then with Mario, where I isolated so badly I stopped using the bathroom, and painted feng shui canvases nailed to a tree in the rain. I turned thirty in September.

## V. The Whispering Trail
*2020–2021 · Ages 30–32*

> *The Whispering Trail of the Dreaming Spirit names the path by which presence continues beyond authorized borders. It is a trail because it is traveled through time, not possessed as a finished doctrine.*

### Taking up the cross without the creed

In early 2020, homeless again, I spent two weeks in **Las Vegas** walking up and down town with a walking stick across my back like a cross. I didn't believe in Jesus. I was taking his words literally anyway: *take up your cross and walk.* Then **Arizona**, to be near my grandmother. I was homeless for a month there and ate out of garbage cans. I called a mental health service whose owner shared my first and middle names, **Justin David Lee Harvey** of Just One Life, and he steadied me through the first months of COVID. I cleaned at Barnes & Noble, lived in a backyard unit until I changed it without permission, and tried twice to reach Mormon Lake. I left everything I'd hauled from that storage unit by the roadside, and I haven't bought random things since. It was my first purification.

### The walk

In **April 2020** I came back to Oregon, lasted a few weeks at Douglas's, and started walking: to **Tillamook** on fourteen dollars of food over four days, one of them fasting, then down the coast toward Los Angeles, for months, up to eighteen hours a day.

The framework says jurisdiction is *the power to decide… what forms of life a system can perceive.* A man walking between towns with no money is a form of life a system perceives as a problem. Every town I entered went on edge, like a movie where a stranger rides in. I felt like a criminal, though walking is not a crime.

And yet the trail was full of relation that no record would ever hold:

- **Shiloh**, a young woman who gave me a ride near Honeyman State Park.
- A man with no arms whom I met along the road.
- **Tim Epps**, a PhD student who gave me his fish sweater and a ride to Trinidad. I slept curled inside that sweater with the torso pulled over my legs.
- A woman in **Brookings** who gave me an old blanket, which I cut down to a scrap because when you walk that much you just fall where you stop.

In a teepee at Fort Bragg I heard a roar, like a big animal, and was terrified. I heard it again in Trinidad, and a third time after I got home, behind my parents' shed. I don't know what it was. The framework uses *ghost* for *what remains active after an official account has declared it absent.* I'm content to leave that roar in the Ghost-Jurisdiction, unexplained and not explained away.

I made it to **Marin County** and couldn't find the Golden Gate Bridge. Whatever had pushed me forward ran out there. Turning north, I collapsed by the road near **Brookings**. The doctor there, Dr. Flowers, didn't write *madness*. She said I was exhausted. One accurate word. From Coos Bay, my mother bought my ticket home.

### Star-seeded words

> *A word becomes star-seeded not through mystical purity but through attention — through remembering where one stands, whom one affects.*

Out there, the prayer from the BYU library became practical. When I was afraid at night with nothing, I looked for one star. It oriented me. That's the most star-seeded sentence I own: borrowed from a prayer book whose origin I can't name, and I won't claim it for any people. I can only say it held.

### Three states and a wheelbarrow

In **December 2020** I went back to Arizona: a men's center in Mesa, McDonald's again, a house of older men (Leroy, Jim, Pascual, and finally a gravedigger named Viper, who moved into my room). By summer I was on the street. A mission acquaintance, **Enrique Gómez**, took me into his tiny house in **Steamboat Springs, Colorado**. I trained jiu-jitsu and worked at Brooklyn's Pizza, then was hospitalized and lost in Denver for a while. I left as the cold came.

In **Utah**, in Springville, I bought a **wheelbarrow**, painted it blue, chalked words on it, loaded it with a cinder block and the landscaping tools I'd bought with my last paycheck, and pushed it north toward Tooele, in honor of a real long-distance wheelbarrow walker. At a closed bank a man called the police on me. I came out of a 7-Eleven to answer their questions and watched, in real time, the thing the framework describes: suspicion *detached from any specific act* and attached *instead to a type of person*. A man with a wheelbarrow for a vehicle must have stolen the wheelbarrow.

In **Tooele** I lasted briefly in a transitional house full of snoring men. They took my wheelbarrow. Two social workers, **Christy and Nadine**, kept in touch and bought my ticket home. Paper again, a bus ticket, doing exactly what paper can do at its best.

## VI. Heart-Off Statehoods and the Entrusted Self
*2022–2025 · Ages 32–36*

> *Procedure can continue after its ethical purpose has been forgotten.*
>
> *Help should not quietly become a permanent claim of ownership. Support must be answerable to the person supported.*

### Floor, factory, butcher shop

In **2022** I slept on my mother's floor on Shadow Lane and worked at McDonald's and the Old Spaghetti Factory, until I took my parents' car to a smoke shop without asking and couldn't tell Rich why. From **September 2022 to January 2023** I washed dishes at a butcher shop in Hillsboro. In **April 2023** I had LASIK. After twenty years of glasses and contacts, it felt like starting over. Soon after, I threw a backpack in my mother's house, and she told me I wasn't welcome.

### Procedure without heart

What followed was a year of systems working exactly as designed, with no one in particular deciding anything.

An old friend, Madison Wellington, gave me a hammock and a key to his workshop. I ate his Girl Scout cookies and lost his trust. A contractor, **Adrian Roman**, saw me hauling sawed-up branches near the Costco and put me to work with his crew. Abel always said *perfectamundo*. I drifted from that too. I found an **abandoned hut** and lived there for months, until I burned blackberry brush in broad daylight. I was charged with trespass and arson, and the arson was dropped. Then came four or five court dates, each costing money I didn't have to reach. I'm honestly not sure how the case ended, which is its own kind of paper ghost, and something I mean to find out.

Back in my mother's backyard into 2024, I made one more walk south, to Florence and Roseburg, and was rescued again by Justin's ticket home and a stranger, **Greg Fort**, who paid for a hotel that saved me. Then I barely left the yard. I used a bucket and carried it to the dumpster, ashamed to be seen. I made AI art and a spreadsheet of my life to age fifty-five, and I talked out loud to myself in a way my parents heard as mania. They set me up in a **storage unit**, and on the second day a camera, the framework's panopticon made literal, saw which unit I went into, and the attendant told me to get out.

I talked my way into the wood shop of **Rob Hinton**, an old Scout leader. I resented the big house I wasn't invited into and said something to his wife ("you're going to have to die") that she reasonably heard as a threat. When he told me to leave, I bolt-cut his attic hatch and hid there for two days before I came to myself. The framework warns against heart-off procedures. It also asks me to see that I'd stopped answering to the people who were trying to help me. Their fear was real, and I gave them reason for it.

My friend **Andy Silver** let me live in his backyard from November 2024 to June 2025.

### The good bad deal

That **November 2024**, I accepted psychiatric oversight: a long-acting injectable antipsychotic, about a year of appointments with a clinician named Dr. Richards, and visits to LifeWorks NW, Sequoia Mental Health, and the Hawthorne Walk-In Clinic. I accepted it partly because of where I was living and whom I depended on. I called it *the good bad deal*.

The framework gives me the most balanced language I've found for this. It *does not deny that people may need support… or that communities may intervene to prevent harm.* It also insists that *guardianship must not erase the voice it is meant to protect.* For me, the medication mostly made me tired and heavier. I felt like a subject of the drug and the company. I also know I wasn't always safe or easy to live near. Both things belong in the record.

### Relation beyond the registry: Capitán

In early 2025 I went to Phoenix chasing a trucking program for people with records, against my care team's wishes. The shelter felt like a war zone. A Pentecostal revival fed me and bought me a sleeping bag. A barista at the Mesa library told me to go to the hospital, and I ended up in Scottsdale for about twelve days, with a second antipsychotic added on top of the injection. My **grandmother** picked me up. It was the last year of her life, and for the first time I truly spent time with her: dinners, walks, her house full of crystals and trinkets, folk magic and faith side by side. I'm grateful we had that.

I missed my bus home because I believed a stranger who said it had come, and I tore myself apart over it. Justin bought another ticket. North of Sacramento the bus stalled in a snowbound pass. I got off with one bag and walked up a line of stopped trucks, knocking on doors. A young trucker named **Valerie**, who told me to call him ***Capitán***, opened his door, fed me, played his music, and drove me nearly to my doorstep in Hillsboro.

I'd gone south wanting to become a trucker. A trucker carried me home. No form recorded it. It's the purest example I have of what the framework calls **power in being**: *authority grounded in relationship rather than domination.*

### A room, a salt line

In **April 2025** I started at **Dick's Hillsboro Hyundai**, moving cars and doing whatever the lot needs. I've worked there full time since, apart from about three months that summer. In **June 2025** I got my own room. A man had died in it, and his belongings came with it. It took me about a year to clear them. I scrubbed it and ran a bar of charcoal soap and a line of salt around the edges, as I once did on my mother's patio.

The framework says a boundary can be *made answerable to the lives it divides.* A salt line isn't a wall against the world. It's a way of saying: this is the small ground I'm entrusted with, and I'll keep it clean.

### The entrusted self

> *A person is sovereign because they are entrusted with themselves while remaining answerable to the lives and places with which they are connected.*

On **July 20, 2025**, I wrote a **Certificate of Sovereign Being**. In **December 2025** I declined to renew the six-month injection and ended ten years of psychiatric oversight.

I used to describe that as getting free of them. The framework helps me say something truer: I'm trying to become **entrusted** with myself. That isn't the same as answering to no one. It means *self-direction and accountable relation.* It means my mother, Rich, my brothers, and my coworkers still get a say in how my choices land on them. It means that if I start to slip, asking for help is not surrender. Autonomy, the framework says, *is not a prize for conformity*. It isn't a license for isolation either.

## VII. Dissolving the False Binary
*2026 · Ages 36–37*

> *Dissolution does not mean flattening every distinction into sameness. It means loosening the hard binary that turns a distinction into a border of moral rank.*

### Breath

In **January 2026** I quit cigarettes. By **April or May** I'd quit vaping. Over the year I also let go of THC gummies and alcohol. I didn't frame it as a problem, just as a decision that they don't add to my life.

The framework calls *truepulse* *the rhythms through which a life remains in relation: breath, care, food, sleep, grief, movement.* For thirteen years, since that first joint in Provo, I'd been putting smoke between myself and my breath. Quitting is the most physical sentence in this book. It's the body saying *I'm here now* without anything in the way.

### Binaries I lived inside

My life has been a series of hard binaries, each one offered to me as the whole truth:

- **Believer / Satanist.** I was the zealot, then the closet beguiler. Neither was me.
- **Sane / mad.** The file said one thing, my certificate says another. I'm a person who has been very lost and is finding his way. Neither word is my name.
- **Straight / gay.** In **July 2026** I came out to my mother as gay over the phone, knowing as I said it that I was trying it on. I put on makeup and went on Grindr, and I came to understand that I'm not. What I learned was how quickly people hand you an identity when you don't know your own. The framework's correction for me is this: being gay is not a script people fall into. For the people who are, it's a source of meaning and belonging, and it wasn't mine to borrow.
- **Rich / poor, elite / ordinary.** I've blamed the elites. I've blamed poor people too, for wanting to steal, for pretending. The framework asks me directly to stop making whole categories of people into explanations for my pain.

### Hunger and the beguiler online

I haven't had sex in about nine years. Over two years I spent around three hundred dollars on porn sites and apps full of fake profiles, and I fell for a **romance scam**: a number traced to Nigeria pretending to be a woman in Ponca City, Oklahoma. I bought Apple gift cards for it. I came through safely. Here the beguiler was real: a deception dressed up as care. What made me vulnerable wasn't a category. It was shame and isolation, the two oldest walls I carry. I've been free of porn for a few months now. I'm trying to understand the hunger without letting shame run it.

### A sign read by another's parallax

> *A gesture… a silence, or a word whose meaning changes with the listener.*

At work I started making the old temple "first penalty" gesture, a hand drawn across the throat, as private resistance. A coworker saw it as a death sign. When my boss asked, I denied it. I was correct about my intention and wrong about everything that mattered: a sign means what it does in the person who receives it. In the framework's language, I'd forgotten that *speech can be practiced so that naming does not become a license to degrade*, and that gestures are speech. I should have owned it and explained it. Next time I will.

### Other things this year

- I clashed with a salesman and told him I wanted to punch him, then dreamed that night that I watched us both from above.
- I told my mother I was quitting to start a walking business. She cried, Rich called, and I stayed.
- I make eighteen dollars an hour. I'm thirty-seven, and some days I feel I'm finishing last among my peers. I owe about twenty thousand dollars in student loans. When I was little I heard my last name as *Maverick*. I'd still rather drive a truck and work on my projects on the side. My uncle says nobody likes a maverick.
- I began learning **Nonviolent Communication** with Rose City NVC: saying what I feel and what I need instead of hinting, stonewalling, or exploding.
- I went back to Falun Dafa's Saturday meetings, because *truth, compassion, forbearance* is a good three-spoke wheel.

### Writing my own operating structure

I've lived inside a lot of frameworks: my father's side's Mormonism, my grandmothers' folk magic with its signs and tokens, Rastafari, Advaita, A.C. Grayling's humanist *The Good Book*, Falun Dafa. This year I concluded I don't need an external one. In **July and August 2026**, with AI's help, I wrote ***The Bill of Intention: The Unified Operating Structure***, seven articles, and ***The Way of the Wheel***, with six spokes: body, food, time, home, other people, and the one I live with.

The framework gives me a warning I want to keep next to the Bill: *a framework that cannot be corrected will harden into the very enclosure it seeks to dissolve.* My Bill of Intention is not a new temple. It's a draft, like this book. It belongs to me, it answers to the people around me, and it can change.

## VIII. Living-Ghost Language

> *It is "ghost" because it carries what the official record cannot fully capture; it is "living" because it remains active, revisable, and embodied.*

Most of this archive was dictated into a phone while walking, biking, sitting on a park bench, or standing in a car lot. The raw transcripts are full of interruptions: helicopters circling overhead, a man in a flag shirt passing by, the wind, an Ioniq 5 that needs moving. And over and over, a sentence I use to bring myself back:

> ***I digress. I continue. I'm here now. I exist.***

That's my living-ghost language. No clinic wrote it, no church taught it, no court entered it into a record. It's what I say when the official descriptions fall away and I need to find the person underneath. It's revisable, embodied, and mine.

So are the songs: Wil's "The Storm" and "You may be a robot." The Dylan and Cash I busked. The *I love beans* cartoon song I hummed inside my little cloud in high school. Abel's *perfectamundo*. Capitán's music in the cab. The prayer about one star. These carry what the files can't.

This whole repository is a counter-record. Not a correction of the official ones (some of those are accurate within their scope) but what the framework calls *refusing to confuse incompleteness with nonexistence.*

## IX. The Circle I Am Learning to Sit In

> *Its first commitment is that no person should be reduced to the system that describes them. Its second is that critique must not reproduce the collective blame it opposes. Its third is that every declaration of unity must leave room for particular lives to speak in their own voices.*

The framework describes a **Dissolution Circle**: a practice, not an institution, with *no central keeper of truth*. It begins with three acts. I'm trying to practice them on my own life.

**1. Listen for the person behind the category.**
Behind *patient* and *defendant* and *transient*, there was a man looking for one star. Behind *ex-wife*, there was Olivia, who laughed when I was cruel because she didn't know what else to do, and who deserved better than I gave. Behind *the Mormons*, there were Jesús Tenorio's family, Heidi Chávez, Kevin Fudge, my grandmother. Behind *my father*, there is a man who taught me a spell of blamelessness, who harmed a child, and who is still my father. I don't have to resolve that to tell the truth about it.

**2. Ask what the record enables and what it erases.**
The diagnosis gave me shelter and took my credibility. The jail record gave me a psych bed and a history. The bus tickets my mother, Christy and Nadine, and Justin bought were paper that carried me home. My father's conviction record erases nothing that should survive; it protects someone. The church records I un-signed freed me from one story but didn't give me another. I had to write that myself.

**3. Return authority to those whose lives are being described, while recognizing that no individual can speak for every member of a community.**
This book is me taking back the authority to describe my own life. It's also not the last word. My mother, Rich, Quinton, Nick, and everyone named here have their own accounts, and theirs are as real as mine. Where I've been wrong about them, I want to hear it.

The Circle, the framework says, *expects error, repair, and disagreement.* So do I.

## Closing Declaration

I was born on paper on September 2, 1989. I was alive before that, in relation, and I'll be alive beyond every file that has tried to hold me since.

I've been misnamed, and I've misnamed others. I've been enclosed, and I've enclosed myself. I've carried an authorized tongue into other people's homes, and I've been a stranger whose wheelbarrow was assumed to be stolen. I've been helped by systems and harmed by them, and I've harmed the people who tried to help me. All of that is the record. None of it is the whole person.

The framework ends with a measure of sovereignty that I want to make my own: *not the power to control every name, but the capacity to live, speak, and enter relation without surrendering one's full reality to another's definition.*

That's the work of the chapters still ahead, *The Deepening* and *The Long View*, which I haven't lived yet. My notes for them say: *take your health seriously now. Pass something on. Redefine success. Welcome complexity. Make peace with unlived lives.* And underneath every chapter, the values that have held, even when I didn't:

**Honesty · Kindness · Courage · Presence · Accountability · Gratitude · Rest · Community**

> *Presence remains. Relation remains.*
> *There's nothing to fix. There's nothing wrong.*
> *I'm here now. I exist. I continue.*
